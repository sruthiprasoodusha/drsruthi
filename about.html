Biography

I am a scientist at heart, trained as both a physicist and an engineer, with a deep fascination for harnessing light as a powerful diagnostic tool. Leading the "Nanoplasmonic and Optoelectronic Sensing (NANOS) at CAU Kiel, my group's research uniquely blends applied optics, materials engineering, and bio/chemical sensing, creating customized nano-plasmonic interfaces and optoelectronic sensor systems that tackle critical issues in medical diagnostics, environmental monitoring, and agro-food safety.

My research is inspired by everyday challenges that significantly impact global health and sustainability, whether rooted in disease, pollution, environmental changes or the safe and sustainable deployment of renewable energy systems.

One of the core mission of mine is to bridge the critical health care gap through patient-centered breath profiling. Currently, in the Alexander von Humboldt Foundation-funded project I lead, my group is developing a Lab-on-Fiber System for on-site breath analysis. This innovative technology offers transformative capabilities for managing respiratory conditions in personalized healthcare settings, enabling routine profiling of breath volatile organic compounds. What drives me most is the lack of a personalized digital breath profiling platform that enables both individuals and clinicians to monitor, predict, and effectively manage health conditions. This mission combines scientific rigor with human empathy, addressing the unmet needs of countless parents and children worldwide.

The recently funded DFG project further strengthens this vision by developing a reference measurement system for breath analysis.

Additionally, I have been contributing to the EU SOILMONITOR project, designing bioinspired chemical assays for the precise detection of soil nutrients in microfluidics, which supports sustainable agriculture.

Looking forward, my vision is to deliver essential, cutting-edge technologies that advance personalized digital healthcare, sustainable energy storage and environmental sustainability. Equally important to me are mentorship, promoting gender equality in science, and fostering inclusive research environments that enable early-career researchers to thrive.

I welcome connections with peers, researchers, clinicians, startups, and industry partners who share this passion for advancing the field. Let's work together to expand the limits of what science can accomplish for society.
